Word2Vec (CBOW)

CBOW (Continuous Bag of Words) is one of the two architectures of Word2Vec.

Its objective is:

Predict the center (target) word using the surrounding (context) words.

Unlike Skip-Gram, where the target word predicts the context words, CBOW uses the surrounding context words to predict the target word.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Example Sentence

The king is a strong man.

Vocabulary

    Index Word
  ------- --------
        1 the
        2 king
        3 is
        4 a
        5 strong
        6 man

Vocabulary Size = 6

Step 4: Hidden Layer (Embedding Layer)

Assume:

-   Vocabulary Size = 6
-   Embedding Size = 4

The hidden layer stores a 6 × 4 embedding matrix.

  Word         D1      D2     D3     D4
  -------- ------ ------- ------ ------
  the        0.15   -0.22   0.61   0.40
  king       0.92    0.81   0.74   0.66
  is         0.10    0.05   0.18   0.12
  a          0.12    0.14   0.09   0.03
  strong     0.75    0.88   0.60   0.52
  man        0.90    0.79   0.71   0.64

This complete table is called the Embedding Matrix (Hidden Layer).

Note

The vectors shown above are illustrative examples for learning purposes.
Real pre-trained Word2Vec models usually use 100–300 dimensions and
learn the values automatically during training.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Advantages of CBOW

Faster to train than Skip-Gram.
Computationally efficient.
Works well with large datasets.
Performs well for frequent words.
Requires less memory and training time.
Produces good-quality word embeddings for common vocabulary.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Disadvantages of CBOW

Less effective for rare words.
Ignores the order of context words (Bag of Words assumption).
Averaging context embeddings may lose some contextual information.
Cannot distinguish different meanings of the same word (polysemy).
