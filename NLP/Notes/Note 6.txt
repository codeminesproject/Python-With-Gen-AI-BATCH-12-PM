N-grams

N-grams are contiguous (consecutive) sequences of N words or tokens extracted 
from a sentence or text. 
They are used in Natural Language Processing (NLP) to capture the relationship 
and context between neighboring words.

Where:

N = 1 → Unigram (single word)
N = 2 → Bigram (two consecutive words)
N = 3 → Trigram (three consecutive words)
N = 4 → Four-gram (four consecutive words)

Example:

Sentence:
The food is not good

Unigram (N = 1)
----------------
The
food
is
not
good

Bigram (N = 2)
--------------
The food
food is
is not
not good

Trigram (N = 3)
---------------
The food is
food is not
is not good

4-gram (N = 4)
--------------
The food is not
food is not good

N-GRAMS EXAMPLE

Example

Sentence 1 (S1):
The food is good

Sentence 2 (S2):
The food is not good

Notice:
Both sentences contain the word "good", but their meanings are different because of the word "not".

------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1: Remove Stop Words
------------------------------------------------------------

Assume "The" and "is" are removed.

S1:
food good

S2:
food not good

------------------------------------------------------------
Unigram (N = 1)
------------------------------------------------------------

Each individual word becomes a feature.

Vocabulary:
[food, not, good]

+----------+------+-----+------+
| Sentence | food | not | good |
+----------+------+-----+------+
| S1       |  1   |  0  |  1   |
| S2       |  1   |  1  |  1   |
+----------+------+-----+------+

Problem:
Both sentences contain the word "good", so the model cannot easily understand that
"not good" has a negative meaning.

------------------------------------------------------------
Bigram (N = 2)
------------------------------------------------------------

Now we consider two consecutive words.

S1 Bigrams:
food good

S2 Bigrams:
food not
not good

Vocabulary:
[food, not, good, food good, food not, not good]

+----------+------+-----+------+-----------+----------+----------+
| Sentence | food | not | good | food good | food not | not good |
+----------+------+-----+------+-----------+----------+----------+
| S1       |  1   |  0  |  1   |     1     |     0    |     0    |
| S2       |  1   |  1  |  1   |     0     |     1    |     1    |
+----------+------+-----+------+-----------+----------+----------+

Why is this better?

The feature "not good" clearly tells the model that the sentence has a negative meaning.

Even though both sentences contain the word "good", the bigram "not good"
preserves the context.

------------------------------------------------------------
Trigram (N = 3)
------------------------------------------------------------

Three consecutive words are considered.

S1:
food good

(No trigram because only two words remain after removing stop words.)

S2:
food not good

Vocabulary:
[food not good]

+----------+---------------+
| Sentence | food not good |
+----------+---------------+
| S1       |       0       |
| S2       |       1       |
+----------+---------------+

The trigram captures the complete phrase, making the meaning even clearer.

------------------------------------------------------------
Comparison
------------------------------------------------------------

+-----------+-------------------------------+-------------------+
| N-gram    | Example                       | Context Preserved |
+-----------+-------------------------------+-------------------+
| Unigram   | food, not, good              | Very little       |
| Bigram    | food good, food not, not good| Better            |
| Trigram   | food not good                | Even better       |
+-----------+-------------------------------+-------------------+

------------------------------------------------------------
Advantages of N-grams
------------------------------------------------------------

1. Preserves the order of words.
2. Captures the context of nearby words.
3. Improves sentiment analysis (e.g., "not good", "very happy").
4. Simple to implement.
5. Often performs better than Bag of Words for text classification.

------------------------------------------------------------
Disadvantages of N-grams
------------------------------------------------------------

1. Vocabulary size grows rapidly as N increases.
2. Produces many sparse (mostly zero) features.
3. Requires more memory and computation.
4. Rare phrases may appear only once, making learning difficult.
5. Large values of N require much more training data.
