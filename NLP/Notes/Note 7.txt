TF-IDF (Term Frequency – Inverse Document Frequency)

Definition

TF-IDF (Term Frequency – Inverse Document Frequency) is a numerical
technique used in Natural Language Processing (NLP) to measure how
important a word is in a document relative to a collection of documents
(corpus).

It combines two measures:

1. Term Frequency (TF)

Formula:

TF = (Number of repetitions of the word in the sentence) / (Total number of words in the sentence)

TF measures how frequently a word appears in a document.

2. Inverse Document Frequency (IDF)

Formula:

IDF = log e( Number of Sentences / Number of Sentences containing the word
)

IDF reduces the importance of words that appear in many documents and
increases the importance of words that appear in fewer documents.

  ---------
  Example
  ---------

Documents

S1 = “good boy” 
S2 = “good girl” 
S3 = “boy girl good”

Vocabulary: good, boy, girl

  -----------------------------
  Step 1: Term Frequency (TF)
  -----------------------------

  Word   S1    S2    S3
  ------ ----- ----- -----
  good   1/2   1/2   1/3
  boy    1/2   0     1/3
  girl   0     1/2   1/3

Explanation: - 
In S1, the word “good” appears once and there are 2 words. TF(good, S1) = 1/2

In S2, the word “girl” appears once and there are 2 words. TF(girl,S2) = 1/2

In S3, each word appears once and there are 3 words. TF = 1/3

  ------------------------------------------
  Step 2: Inverse Document Frequency (IDF)
  ------------------------------------------

Total number of documents = 3

Document Frequency (DF)

good -> appears in S1, S2, S3 = 3 
boy -> appears in S1, S3 = 2 
girl -> appears in S2, S3 = 2

IDF(good) = log(3/3) = 0

IDF(boy) = log(3/2) = 0.1761

IDF(girl) = log(3/2) = 0.1761

  ----------------
  Step 3: TF-IDF
  ----------------

TF-IDF = TF × IDF

  Word   S1      S2      S3
  ------ ------- ------- --------
  good   0       0       0
  boy    0.088   0       0.0587
  girl   0       0.088   0.0587

  ------------
  Conclusion
  ------------

-   TF measures the frequency of a word within a document.
-   IDF measures how unique a word is across all documents.
-   TF-IDF gives higher scores to important words that occur frequently
    in one document but not in all documents.
-   Since “good” appears in every document, its IDF becomes 0, making
    its TF-IDF score 0.
-   The words “boy” and “girl” are more informative because they appear
    in fewer documents, so they receive higher TF-IDF scores.

Advantages and Disadvantages of TF-IDF

Advantages

1.  Intuitive

-   TF-IDF is easy to understand and implement.
-   It calculates the importance of a word using simple mathematical
    formulas.

2.  Fixed Size Representation

-   The feature vector has a fixed size equal to the vocabulary size.
-   Every document is represented using the same number of features.

3.  Captures Word Importance

-   TF-IDF assigns higher weights to important words.
-   Common words that appear in almost every document receive lower
    weights.
-   This helps distinguish one document from another.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Disadvantages

1.  Sparsity Still Exists

-   Most values in the TF-IDF matrix are zero because every document
    contains only a few words from the complete vocabulary.
-   This results in a sparse matrix, increasing memory usage for large
    datasets.

2.  OOV (Out of Vocabulary)

-   TF-IDF cannot handle words that were not present in the training
    vocabulary.
-   Any new or unseen word is ignored during transformation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Summary

Advantages - Easy to understand and implement. - Fixed-size feature
vector. - Captures the importance of words effectively.

Disadvantages - Produces sparse matrices. - Cannot handle
Out-of-Vocabulary (OOV) words.
