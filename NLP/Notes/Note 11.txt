Word2Vec (Skip-Gram) - Complete Notes

1. What is Skip-Gram?

Skip-Gram is one of the two Word2Vec architectures.

Its objective is:

Predict the surrounding (context) words using the center (target) word.

Unlike CBOW, where context words predict the target word, Skip-Gram does
the opposite.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

2. Example Sentence

“The king is a strong man.”

Vocabulary

    Index Word
  ------- --------
        1 the
        2 king
        3 is
        4 a
        5 strong
        6 man

Vocabulary Size = 6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

3. One-Hot Encoding

  Word     One-Hot Vector
  -------- ----------------
  the      [1,0,0,0,0,0]
  king     [0,1,0,0,0,0]
  is       [0,0,1,0,0,0]
  a        [0,0,0,1,0,0]
  strong   [0,0,0,0,1,0]
  man      [0,0,0,0,0,1]

------------------------------------------------------------------------

4. Skip-Gram Training

Sentence:

The king is a strong man

Assume Window Size = 5 (2 words before and 2 words after)

Center Word (Input)

is

Context Words (Output)

the king a strong

Training Pairs

  Input (Center Word)   Output (Context Word)
  --------------------- -----------------------
  is                    the
  is                    king
  is                    a
  is                    strong

Instead of one training example, Skip-Gram creates multiple training
pairs.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

5. Input Layer

Input Word

is

One-Hot Vector

[0,0,1,0,0,0]

------------------------------------------------------------------------

6. Hidden Layer (Embedding Layer)

Assume

Vocabulary Size = 6

Embedding Size = 4

Hidden Layer (Embedding Matrix)

  Word         D1      D2     D3     D4
  -------- ------ ------- ------ ------
  the        0.15   -0.22   0.61   0.40
  king       0.92    0.81   0.74   0.66
  is         0.10    0.05   0.18   0.12
  a          0.12    0.14   0.09   0.03
  strong     0.75    0.88   0.60   0.52
  man        0.90    0.79   0.71   0.64

When the input word is “is”, the hidden layer selects the row
corresponding to “is”.

Embedding of “is”

[0.10, 0.05, 0.18, 0.12]

------------------------------------------------------------------------

7. Output Layer

Using the embedding of “is”, the network predicts every surrounding
word.

Example probabilities

  Word       Probability
  -------- -------------
  the               0.85
  king              0.91
  is                0.02
  a                 0.88
  strong            0.82
  man               0.05

The model learns that “the”, “king”, “a”, and “strong” are likely
context words for the center word “is”.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

8. Training Process

Initially, the embedding matrix contains random values.

Example

  Word       D1      D2     D3     D4
  ------ ------ ------- ------ ------
  is       0.21   -0.15   0.32   0.18

After training on millions of sentences

  Word       D1     D2     D3     D4
  ------ ------ ------ ------ ------
  is       0.10   0.05   0.18   0.12

These values are learned using backpropagation.

10. Advantages

-   Learns high-quality word embeddings.
-   Performs well for rare words.
-   Works well on small datasets.
-   Captures semantic relationships effectively.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

11. Disadvantages

-   Slower than CBOW.
-   Creates many training pairs.
-   Requires more computation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

12. CBOW vs Skip-Gram

  Feature         CBOW            Skip-Gram
  --------------- --------------- ---------------
  Input           Context Words   Center Word
  Output          Center Word     Context Words
  Speed           Faster          Slower
  Rare Words      Moderate        Better
  Small Dataset   Good            Excellent
  Large Dataset   Excellent       Good

14. Summary

-   Skip-Gram predicts surrounding words from a center word.
-   It uses one-hot encoding as input.
-   The hidden layer stores the embedding matrix.
-   Each word is represented by a dense embedding vector.
-   The embeddings are learned through backpropagation.
-   Skip-Gram is slower than CBOW but generally learns better
    representations for infrequent words.
